# Ecuador: Political, Social, and Legal Developments (2010–2019)

## Overview
Between March 2010 and July 2019, Ecuador experienced a period of intense political and social transformation. This era included an attempted police coup, growing authoritarian tendencies under President [Rafael Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Correa), a sharp political realignment under his successor [Lenín Moreno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Len%C3%ADn_Moreno), and major international events such as the termination of asylum for [Julian Assange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Assange).

---

## Timeline of Key Events

### 2010
**September 2010 – Police Revolt / Attempted Coup**  
Police units and some military factions revolted against a law reforming public-sector benefits. President Rafael Correa was assaulted with tear gas, confined to a police hospital, and later rescued by the army. The incident was widely described domestically and internationally as an attempted coup.  
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Ecuador_crisis

---

### 2011

**April 2011 – Diplomatic Crisis with the United States**  
Ecuador expelled the U.S. ambassador following leaked diplomatic cables alleging police corruption, triggering a major diplomatic rift.  
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuador%E2%80%93United_States_relations

**May 2011 – Constitutional Referendum**  
Voters approved a referendum expanding presidential influence over the judiciary and increasing regulation of the media, strengthening executive power.  
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Ecuadorian_referendum_and_popular_consultation

---

### 2012

**March 2012 – Indigenous and Environmental Mobilization**  
The “March for Water, Life, and the Dignity of Peoples” highlighted national opposition to extractive policies, mining expansion, and oil development in indigenous territories.  
Source: https://www.culturalsurvival.org/news/marching-water-life-and-dignity-indigenous-peoples-ecuador

---

### 2013
**February 2013 – Correa Reelected**  
Rafael Correa won re-election, consolidating the “Citizens’ Revolution” and extending his presidency until 2017.  
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Ecuadorian_general_election

**November 2013 – Rising Authoritarianism**  <br>



Tensions escalated with indigenous organizations such as CONAIE and civil society groups over mining, oil extraction, limits on protest, and pressure on independent media.  
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_Indigenous_Nationalities_of_Ecuador

---

### 2015
**June 2015 – Mass Protests**  
Large-scale demonstrations erupted in response to proposed inheritance and capital-gains taxes and concerns over increasing concentration of presidential power.  
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Ecuador

**December 2015 – Removal of Term Limits**  
Constitutional changes eliminated presidential term limits, theoretically allowing Correa to run indefinitely, though he later declined to stand in the 2017 election.  
Source: https://constitutionnet.org/news/chronicle-amendment-foretold-eliminating-presidential-term-limits-ecuador

---

### 2017
**February 2017 – Election of Lenín Moreno**  
Lenín Moreno, Correa’s former vice-president, was elected president, initially pledging continuity with the leftist project.  
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Ecuadorian_general_election

**December 2017 – Corruption Investigations**  
Corruption cases advanced against former officials linked to Correa’s administration, including investigations related to the [Odebrecht scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odebrecht_corruption_scandal).  
Source: https://www.bbc.com/news/world-latin-america-42346881

---

### 2018
**4 February 2018 – National Referendum**  
A referendum reinstated presidential term limits and barred Correa from returning to the presidency, deeply fracturing the ruling movement.  
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Ecuadorian_referendum_and_popular_consultation

**July 2018 – Correa Convictions Advanced**  
An Ecuadorian court advanced convictions in a corruption and kidnapping case involving Rafael Correa, ordering his arrest. Correa remained abroad and denied the charges.  
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Correa#Legal_issues, https://www.latinorebels.com/2018/07/06/correa-2/

---

### 2019
**March 2019 – IMF Agreement**  
Ecuador finalized a $4.2 billion loan agreement with the [International Monetary Fund (IMF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund), conditioned on austerity measures.  
Source: https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2019/03/11/ecuador-pr1972-imf-executive-board-approves-eff-for-ecuador

**April 2019 – Assange Asylum Revoked**  
The Ecuadorian government revoked Julian Assange’s asylum in its London embassy, leading to his immediate arrest by British police.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Assange#Arrest

**October 2019 – Mass Protests Over Fuel Subsidy Cuts**  
The government enacted decree 883 ending four decades of fuel subsidies, triggering a sudden doubling of gasoline and diesel prices.  
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Ecuadorian_protests
